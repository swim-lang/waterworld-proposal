    1 Start here

    A lot of people making things. A few too many versions of the brand.

    Jenny, your answers helped clarify the assignment. Water World already has a recognizable logo and decades of brand equity. The practical challenge is getting everyone creating materials to work from the same visual decisions.

    Different contributors are using different fonts, styles, and treatments. Your marketing team manages direction and approvals, but there isn't a clear visual system everyone can reliably work from.

    We'll build that system around the strongest parts of the existing brand, with particular attention to typography. The work will support your primary audience of Colorado and regional families while allowing campaigns for groups, tourists, and young adults to adjust their tone and emphasis.

    The standards should help someone choose a font, build a layout, brief a vendor, or review an ad with more confidence. They should also improve the materials themselves: clearer hierarchy, better relationships between elements, and a more consistent expression of the brand you already have.

    How each phase works

    01Understand the starting point

    We'll begin with a kickoff workshop and focused input from marketing, the internal designer, a social/content contributor, and an agency representative. We'll review a representative collection of current and recent materials: website graphics, organic and paid social, display ads, email, outdoor, print, signage, video, promotional pieces, merchandise, and work produced by both staff and partners.

    The audit will identify what is already consistent, what keeps changing, and where people need clearer direction. We'll distinguish assets with established equity from treatments that could benefit from refinement. Water World's existing logo is the starting point for its usage standards.

    We'll also review available historical materials. Nostalgia can inform our choices when it connects the work to something meaningful and familiar. We'll consider how those references read to first-time visitors as well as people who grew up with the park.

    02Develop the system

    Typography comes early. We'll establish clear roles for primary, secondary, and any supporting or display fonts, including headlines, subheads, body copy, and calls to action. Recommendations will consider readability, accessibility, licensing, web use, and practical availability in Canva and Adobe. We'll specify substitutes where the main fonts aren't available, including email environments.

    We'll formalize logo usage and the color palette, then develop a focused graphic language. That may include shapes, patterns, textures, illustration direction, icons, frames, and backgrounds, with water or Colorado references where they serve the work. Existing mascot or character assets can be considered if supplied; a new mascot program would be a separate scope.

    We'll present two focused visual approaches using representative applications, then develop one selected direction. Exploration will draw from the audit and useful historical references, keeping the system practical for today's production needs.

    03Put it to work

    We'll test the selected direction across digital and physical applications, including short and long headlines, different photography, seasonal promotions, and informational messages.

    We'll define the elements that stay consistent and the elements contributors can vary. A social post, billboard, and operational sign have different jobs; the system needs to account for those differences.

    Photography and video direction will cover subject matter, guest representation, attractions and lifestyle imagery, composition, movement, lighting, color treatment, authentic and staged content, drone imagery, titles, overlays, and social-first video. Examples will show how professionally produced and internally captured content can work together.

    Basic voice guidance will address personality, headline style, calls to action, humor, and the differences between promotional, informational, and operational messages.

    04Make the handoff useful

    We'll organize the final standards and assets so staff and partners can find the current files and understand how to use them. The guide will include practical examples and common mistakes alongside specifications.

    We'll conduct a 60-minute walkthrough for staff and agency partners, with time for questions. The transition plan will prioritize social, paid advertising, email, website creative, agency-produced work, and internal templates. Permanent signage, remaining print stock, merchandise, and environmental graphics can transition as replacement becomes appropriate.

    5 Try the tool

    Some design decisions only need to be made once

    A recurring promotion shouldn't require someone to rebuild the layout or work out the type hierarchy every time.

    We'll show how an approved design can become an editable tool: change the headline, swap the photo, update the details, and keep the underlying design decisions in place. For the right tasks, that gives your team more control and a quicker starting point. It also lets us build tools around the way you work, rather than asking every contributor to become a designer.

    Try a sample: edit the headline, image, and event details to see how a coded template responds.

    Clear standards can also give AI-assisted workflows a better foundation. Over time, an approved collection of rules, assets, templates, and examples could help your team prepare variations, check work against the standards, and get routine production tasks moving faster.

    7 Your questions

    Eight good questions, answered

    1Based on your experience, what separates a useful brand standards guide from one that ultimately sits unused?

    A useful guide answers the questions people encounter while making things. It tells someone which font to use, how much flexibility a campaign has, where the approved assets live, and what to do when a preferred font or format isn't available.

    We'll pair the specifications with realistic applications, editable templates, common mistakes, and a walkthrough. We'll test whether contributors can find what they need and use it. The guide should reduce uncertainty in everyday work.

    2How would you approach refining an established brand without making it feel like a complete rebrand?

    We'll begin by identifying the elements that already carry equity. Jenny identified the logo as the strongest recognizable asset, so our work starts by clarifying its use and improving the system around it.

    Historical materials can inform typography, graphic choices, or attitude where those references are useful today. We'll be selective: each choice needs to work for current audiences and production needs. Any recommendation for a significant logo modification would be explained and scoped separately.

    3How do you develop a visual identity that remains recognizable while allowing enough flexibility for seasonal campaigns, events and promotions?

    We'll define the constants: approved logo use, typography roles, palette relationships, and a focused graphic language. Then we'll show where campaigns can change, including image choice, layout, emphasis, supporting colors, and expressive details.

    Examples will demonstrate those choices across family, group, tourist, and young-adult communications. Contributors will have clear boundaries and enough room to make work appropriate to the message.

    4How would you approach Water World's current typography inconsistency?

    We'll inventory the fonts and treatments currently in use, identify practical constraints, and establish a small hierarchy with defined roles. We'll test it with real headlines, promotional details, body copy, calls to action, and viewing conditions.

    The recommendations will address Canva and Adobe availability, web and email compatibility, licensing, readable sizing, and substitutes. Clear instructions will help the internal team and partners make the same choices across channels.

    5How do you account for the different needs of professional designers, marketing staff, social media teams and external agencies when developing brand standards?

    The same system needs different levels of explanation. Designers and agencies need precise specifications and adaptable source assets. Marketing and social contributors need quick guidance, examples, and templates for recurring work. Vendors need production specifications and correct exports.

    We'll organize those resources around common tasks and test them with representative users. A custom template tool could later support constrained recurring tasks, while the base package remains usable in Canva and Adobe.

    6What steps do you take to ensure a new visual system can be practically implemented across an organization?

    We'll involve the people producing and approving materials early, test the system on representative applications, and resolve platform and licensing questions before handoff.

    We'll deliver organized assets, train staff and partners, and identify immediate priorities for new work. Longer-lived physical materials can transition at sensible replacement points. We'll recommend a named internal owner for the approved asset collection and a clear process for handling exceptions and updates.

    7What involvement would you need from Water World staff during the project?

    We'll need a marketing lead to coordinate materials, combine feedback, and confirm decisions. The internal designer, social/content contributor, and agency representative should provide focused input at discovery and review the practical applications. Park leadership can participate at the checkpoints agreed during kickoff.

    We propose a 90-minute kickoff, one direction presentation, two consolidated direction feedback rounds, one guide/template correction round, and a 60-minute training session. Access to current assets and feedback within two business days will support the December 23 target.

    8Are there components you believe should be added to or removed from the proposed scope? Please explain.

    We recommend emphasizing usability testing, substitute-font guidance, asset organization, and staff/partner training. Those details help turn the standards into everyday working tools.

    Jenny's clarification supports keeping extensive consumer research and existing asset/signage refreshes outside this engagement. Basic voice direction is useful, while comprehensive positioning would require its own scope.

    We also recommend exploring a coded template tool and, eventually, a focused brand-aware AI workflow as optional next steps. The standards provide a useful foundation for those tools; each extension should earn its place by solving a real production problem.
